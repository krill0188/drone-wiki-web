# Wire-Driven Robotic Fish

Wire-driven robotic fish is a biomimetic aquatic robot that employs a wire-driven actuation system based on a 2-degree-of-freedom crank-slider mechanism to achieve high swimming speed and agile maneuverability. It decouples propulsion from steering, overcoming the speed-maneuverability trade-off seen in conventional robotic fish designs.

## 개요

Robotic fish utilizing the Body-Caudal Fin (BCF) locomotion pattern are widely studied for environmental monitoring and biological surveys. Traditional motor-driven actuation methods present a trade-off: multi-servo systems allow precise body curvature control but limit swimming speed, while wire or rod-driven systems using brushless motors achieve higher oscillation and speed but suffer from poor maneuverability and difficulty in smooth turning. The presented design resolves this by implementing a 2-DoF crank-slider mechanism that physically decouples propulsion and steering functions. This architecture enables both high swimming speed and agile maneuvering in a single platform.

## 핵심 특징

- **2-DoF crank-slider mechanism**: Propulsion and steering are decoupled via a two-degree-of-freedom crank-slider system.
- **High swimming speed**: Wire-driven actuation enables higher oscillation frequencies and swimming speeds compared to multi-servo approaches.
- **Agile maneuverability**: Decoupled control allows for smooth turning and improved steering capability, addressing the weakness of conventional wire-driven designs.
- **Biomimetic design**: Incorporates an elastic skeleton and waterproof structure to mimic biological fish morphology.
