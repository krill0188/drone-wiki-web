## Abstract

Quantifying landslide-induced topographic change requires precise comparison of pre- and post-event terrain data. When the datasets differ in acquisition date, sensor type, spatial resolution, and positional accuracy, measurement error may be misinterpreted as real change. We reconstructed the pre-event terrain from 1:5,000 digital-map contours and spot heights using a triangulated irregular network (TIN), generated the post-event DTM from UAV LiDAR, and applied geomorphic change detection (GCD). Residual elevation differences over stable roads had a mean of -0.04 m and a standard deviation of 0.84 m; the mean vertical offset was therefore negligible relative to the dispersion.
